# ADR-0020: LoRa radio modules

Status: accepted (19 November 2026).

## Context
LoRa reaches kilometres without a network: Meshtastic and the LoRa firmwares of Bruce-class devices are why many people buy an
ESP32 handheld with a module. hhv2 has no LoRa chip, but PalanuOS is board-agnostic: the next board, or a module on a header,
should work without system changes.

## Decision
- **A LoRa module is a radio device.** `PAL_DEVRADIO_LORA` joins the radio types, and a radio device may carry `lora`, the
  driver's ops (`pal/lora.h`: configure, send, receive with RSSI and SNR). The system finds the first one (`pal_lora_device`).
- **Settings, not code, pick the band.** `lora.freq`, `lora.sf`, `lora.bw`, `lora.cr` and `lora.power` are settings (868.1 MHz,
  SF9, 125 kHz, 4/5, 14 dBm by default); the owner sets the frequency of their region. The sync word is the private one (0x12):
  PalanuOS devices hear each other, LoRaWAN gateways do not. Raw point-to-point packets only: no LoRaWAN, no mesh yet.
- **The first driver is the SX126x** (SX1262 and its siblings, the chip of most current modules), portable C over the board's SPI
  bus device and pin devices (`pal/sx126x.h`): TCXO on DIO3 and the antenna switch on DIO2 when the module has them, image
  calibration for the band, the PA configuration of the SX1262 or SX1261.
- **The simulator has a module over UDP** (`--lora PORT:PEER`): two simulators with crossed ports exchange packets, a receiver set
  to other parameters hears nothing, and sending takes the real time on air.
- On the device: `lora` (info, set, send, listen) and `pal.lora_send`/`pal.lora_recv` in Lua.

## Consequences
- The driver follows the datasheet and is tested against a pretend chip (`test_lora`: the bytes of the configuration, a packet
  sent and received with RSSI and SNR, a CRC error, the time on air); it has not talked to real silicon. The first board with a
  module proves it.
- CC1101 (sub-GHz OOK/FSK) and nRF24 can follow as further radio drivers on the same device seam.
